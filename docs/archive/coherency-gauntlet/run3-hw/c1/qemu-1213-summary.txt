## Method
- QEMU command: the `make test` recipe (`make -n test`), with `-accel tcg,tb-size=128,thread=<single|multi>`,
  a QMP socket and, from single run04 on, `-gdb tcp:localhost:<port>`. It is wrapped in
  `systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=3G -p CPUQuota=200% timeout 120` with `-m 1G`, and
  a fresh 4G sparse SD image per run (as `make test` creates it). One QEMU at a time. Single and multi
  were interleaved (single NN, multi NN, ...). The scripts are in `qemu-1213-sampler.sh.txt`.
- Sampler: at the first `[test_wait] iso_latency_isolated: TIMEOUT` line (or `iso_latency_normal`), it
  takes 10 samples 200 ms apart of every vCPU through QMP `info registers`. Each sample gives the PC with
  its addr2line symbol and the instructions at pc-4 and pc (objdump), plus `current_task[cpu]` (BSS
  array at 0x407cd130, read with `x`) with that task's name and state. From single run04 on, each sample
  also records X30 with its symbol and a full register dump at sample 01. After the 10 samples, one
  gdbstub read takes pc, sp, ESR_EL1, FAR_EL1, ELR_EL1, SPSR_EL1, SP_EL1 and a backtrace for every vCPU.
  Multi run02 and run03 used the earlier sampler (PC, symbol, instructions, current_task only).
- Runs: the brief's 5 + 5 (single and multi run01-05). Extra runs 06-15 per mode were added to get
  gdbstub reads in a stall and a larger single-versus-multi count. Ten multi "trace" runs
  (`qemu-1213-multi-trace-run01..10.txt`) attached gdb from boot with a breakpoint on the
  current-EL/SPx synchronous vector, to catch the first fault of a stall.
- Discarded or not counted (all recorded): the first multi run01 attempt at 06:59 did not start
  (QMP socket path over 108 bytes). A sampler self-test (`qemu-1213-sampler-selftest-multi-run99.txt`,
  trigger forced on a later line, no stall) checked QMP reads before the series resumed at single run02.

## What the stalled runs show (6 of 6 stalls are thread=multi)
1. **Does CPU 1's PC move?** It moves, but only inside a fault or panic path, never through task or
   scheduler code. Over the 10 samples:
   - In 5 of 6 stalls (multi run02, 03, 07, 12, 15) CPU 1 alternated between two addresses:
     - 0x40010a00, `exception_vectors+0x200` (the current-EL/SPx synchronous vector, `b el1_sync`);
     - 0x40010f94, `el1_sync+4` (`stp x0, x1, [sp]` right after `sub sp, sp, #0x320`).
   - In 1 of 6 (multi run14) CPU 1 sat in `panic` (0x40042b98 and 0x40042b9c), after the serial line
     `task_exit: schedule returned!` (Task ID 180, `lat_iso`, CPU 1; `qemu-1213-multi-run14.txt`).
   - CPU 1's PSTATE was 0x3c5 (EL1h, D, A, I and F all masked) in the 5 loop runs and 0x600003c5 in
     run14.
2. **Function and syndrome.** The gdbstub reads in multi run07, 12 and 15 all gave the same syndrome:
   - ESR_EL1 0x96000045: data abort taken at the same EL, write, level-1 translation fault.
   - FAR_EL1 equal to SP_EL1, and ELR_EL1 = `el1_sync+4`.
   - So the stack push of the exception entry itself faults, the vector re-enters, `sub sp, #0x320`
     moves SP down, and the loop repeats with all exceptions masked.
   - SP_EL1 is congruent to 0 modulo 0x320 in 6 of 6 readings: 2 per run in run07, 12 and 15, at
     sample 01 and at the gdb read, between 1.88 and 6.08 million 0x320-byte steps below 0. That fits
     an SP of exactly 0 when the loop started. A valid stack top would not have faulted.
   - The other registers on CPU 1 at sample 01 were identical in 3 of 3 (run07, 12, 15). They are the
     image task_create writes for a new task, as `switch_to` loads it:
     - x19 = x0 = `latency_task_entry`;
     - x20 = 0, x29 = 0;
     - x30 = `task_entry_wrapper` (its entry address, not a return address);
     - x2 = 0x407ce510 (= task 0x407ce4f0 + 0x20, the context `switch_to` restored from);
     - x3 = 0x80 (the fresh context's DAIF, the last word `switch_to` loads).
   - `task_entry_trampoline`'s first instruction is `stp x29, x30, [sp, #-32]!`. With SP = 0 that
     store faults, and a faulting pre-indexed store does not update SP.
3. **Which task is RUNNING on CPU 1.**
   - The suite's own re-poll lines give the first timed-out isolated task state 1 (RUNNING) at the
     bound in 6 of 6 stalls, and still 1 at +1000 ms. Every later one is 0 (READY).
   - `current_task[1]` was 0x407ce4f0 in every sample of every stall. That struct address held later
     `lat_iso` and `lat_norm` tasks (READY, ids advancing between samples): the test destroyed the
     first one and task_create reused the same slot.
   - In multi run14, `current_task[1]` named `lat_iso` READY while CPU 1 was in `panic`.
4. **The waiter's poll loop on CPU 0 executes `yield()` between polls, not WFE and not WFI.**
   - CPU 0 ran task `main` (RUNNING) in 60 of 60 samples. Its PC was always `timer_get_count`
     (0x4002fda8, `mrs x0, cntpct_el0`).
   - Its X30 was in `test_wait_until_v` in 40 of 40 samples that recorded it: 0x4007d1c4 in the main
     poll loop, 0x4007d288 in the re-poll loop. The gdb backtrace was
     `timer_get_count <- test_wait_until_v <- test_isolated_core_latency` in 4 of 4.
   - That loop's code calls `bl yield` (0x4007d1d4) on every pass and contains no WFE or WFI.
   - The PC landing on the CNTPCT read in 60 of 60 samples is probably where QEMU stops a vCPU for
     a QMP request; hypothesis, not tested.
   - CPUs 2 and 3 were halted in WFI in `idle_task_func` (instruction at pc-4 `wfi`) in every sample.
5. **Thread mode.**
   - Stalls: single 0 of 15, multi 6 of 15.
   - With gdb attached and a breakpoint inserted (multi): 0 of 10. Attaching gdb changes the timing
     enough to hide the stall.
#
## Outcome
The brief's two classes do not fit cleanly.
- The stall needs parallel vCPUs (thread=multi 6/15, thread=single 0/15), and CPU 1's PC moves.
- But it is not a TCG scheduling artifact:
  - CPU 1 is not runnable at all. It is in a recursive EL1 data abort on a stack pointer that started
    at 0, in 5 of 6 stalls, or in `panic("task_exit: schedule returned!")`, in 1 of 6.
  - The waiter yields between polls.
- It is also not a lock, WFE or WFI hang.
- Measured outcome: a kernel fault on CPU 1 that is exposed only by truly parallel vCPUs. CPU 1
  switched into a task whose saved SP was 0 while its x19, x20, x29, x30 and DAIF already held a new
  task's initial values.
#
## Hypothesis (untested; recorded, not worked)
- The task slot is reused while CPU 1 is still leaving the previous task in that slot.
- The test calls `task_destroy(t)` as soon as it sees TASK_TERMINATED, and `task_create` reuses the
  slot at once. CPU 1's `schedule()` after `task_exit` also holds the old task as `rq->zombie` and
  calls `task_destroy` on it at its next `schedule()`, so there are two destroyers for one task.
- A read of the slot's context concurrent with `task_create` fits the measured registers. The
  compiled store order in `task_create_with_priority` is:
  1. the context is zeroed byte by byte;
  2. x19/x20 (`stp ... [x4, #32]`);
  3. x29/x30 (`stp xzr, x1, [x4, #112]`);
  4. sp (`str x3, [x4, #128]`);
  5. daif (`str x2, [x4, #672]`).
- `switch_to` reads sp first, then x19..x30, then daif.
- Under that interleaving, CPU 1 would see sp = 0 together with a fresh x19/x20/x29/x30 and DAIF.
- multi run14's `task_exit: schedule returned!` fits the same race: `schedule()` on CPU 1 found
  `current` no longer TERMINATED and returned to it.
- Not tested; the gdb-from-boot trace runs (0 of 10) did not catch a first fault.
