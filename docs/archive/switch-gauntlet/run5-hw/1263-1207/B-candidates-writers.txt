#1207: where the damaged list heads are, and what on the Jetson kexec path could write there.
Code reading and existing captures only (fix/pmm-coalesce 647c2c33). Nothing here was tested on hardware.
"Not verified" marks what was not read in code or cannot be read from a capture.

A. What the captures say about the blocks

1. The two halts at f55c5293 (B-kexec-suite-f55c5293-boot01.txt, -boot05.txt): the order-7 list had 30 blocks at boot
   and the panic count is 29; the order-5 list had 50 and the count is 48 ("Free list distribution" in the same files).
   The list therefore ended after one pop (boot01) and after at most two (boot05): the damaged block was the head of
   that list, or the block after it, when test_exhaustion_recovery reached it.
2. No capture prints block addresses (the new verifier does). What can be said from the layout: free lists are LIFO and
   pmm_init adds regions in ascending address order (kernel/mm/pmm.c, the four pmm_add_region_split calls in pmm_init),
   so the head of a mid-order list at boot is the highest-addressed fragment of that order, and those fragments are the
   gaps between the GA10B hand-off reservations in region 3 (0x100000000-0x240000000). The first blocks handed out show
   it: task stacks (order 6, the list head at the time) sit among the reserves in every boot, e.g. boot01 main stack
   0x13c240000 with the mnist hand-off page at 0x13c5ae000, pushbuf 0x13c600000, qmd_pool 0x13ca40000, channel inst
   block 0x13caf7000; boot05 main stack 0x129e00000 with the inst block at 0x129410000.
   Inference: the damaged heads are pages that Linux had in use shortly before kexec, next to (and interleaved with) the
   buffers the GPU hand-off reserves. Not shown: the address of either damaged block.
3. Why the reserve count differs between boots (189 and 190 in B-kexec-chk-oldalloc-17daedda-boot01,02.txt): the
   channel-VM reservation coalesces adjacent pages into runs (vm_reserve_add / vm_reserve_flush,
   kernel/gpu/nvidia/ga10b_handoff_reserve.c:808-837), and the number of runs depends on how Linux's allocator placed the
   pages in that boot: "910 pages in 80 ranges" / "917 pages in 55 ranges" in one boot, 86 / 50 in the other; the ten
   f55c5293 boots have 80 to 85 and 64 to 78. The page totals (910, 917, 15 and 17 gr_ctx extents) are the same in every
   boot. Free pages at boot differ by up to 73 pages between boots (1803607 to 1803680), which is the overlap between
   reservations varying, not shown to be anything else.

B. Candidate writers, most plausible first

1. The xHCI controller, left running by Linux on purpose, until SLM-OS halts it.
   - scripts/jetson-kexec-slmos.sh keeps it alive across kexec: step [5/9] holds the xusb clocks and power gates
     (line 1038), [6/9] pins tegra-xusb runtime PM (1091), [7/9] loads arm_smmu_noshutdown so the xusb SMMU mapping
     stays an identity mapping (1151-1178), then stashes slot hand-off data (1183-1190).
   - SLM-OS runs pmm_init at kernel/src/main.c:418 and xhci_init at main.c:874; the controller is first stopped in
     xhci_init (xhci_halt, kernel/drivers/usb/xhci/xhci.c:1165, called at :2176). From kexec until then it runs with
     Linux's DCBAA, device contexts, event ring, scratchpad buffers and any queued transfer buffers, all Linux pages that
     the PMM has on its free lists (no pmm_user_reserve_add for any of them; the only callers are
     ga10b_handoff_reserve.c and kernel/src/boot_models.c:117).
   - SLM-OS itself reads Linux's DCBAA and device contexts in RAM after kexec (xhci_log_inherited_dcbaa, xhci.c:187-245),
     and the suite captures print "stale port ... skipping reset and reusing retained slot1 state".
   - What it would write: event TRBs into Linux's event ring, slot/endpoint state into output device contexts,
     received data into queued IN buffers (a USB Ethernet adapter is attached: "cdc_ecm" lines in the captures), scratchpad
     pages. A device context and an event-ring segment start at offset 0 of a page. Not verified: that any of those
     pages is a free-list head, or what the written bytes are. Run 5's 0x0007000000000000 read as two 32-bit words is
     0x00000000, 0x00070000; in an xHCI Slot Context the second word's bits 23:16 are the root hub port number. That is
     a resemblance, not evidence.
   - Fits: kexec only; only in some boots (needs an event in the window and the page at a block head); the write lands
     any time after pmm_init, long before the PMM tests.
2. The GPU and its firmware (FECS, PMU), also left running on purpose (step [4/9], the channel-inherit helpers, line
   1031; [8.5/9] force-on, 1232).
   - Reserved: channel inst blocks (ga10b_handoff_reserve.c:185, :748), the hand-off page (:314), USERD, GPFIFO, pushbuf,
     semaphore, shader, cbuf, qmd_pool (:360-377), pipeline ops (:458), per-op shaders (:537), gr_ctx extents (:595, :617),
     weights extents (:681, :709), every page mapped in each channel's VM (:812).
   - Not reserved: the runlist buffer; every capture prints "runlist submit_base reads poison ... Skipping" (:285-297 is
     the reservation that is skipped). The GPU reads a runlist; not verified whether anything writes it.
   - Not verified: memory nvgpu allocates outside a channel VM that the GPU or PMU firmware writes (PMU queues and
     super-surface, FECS trace buffer, MMU fault buffers, the second helper's buffers). The earlier clobber of this kind
     was the reverse direction (#1029: unreserved GMMU tables given free-list links).
3. SLM-OS code that writes through a stale or foreign pointer after pmm_init. Not surveyed beyond the PMM's callers:
   the QEMU full suite with PMM_CHECK=ON shows no free over a free page and no order larger than the allocation
   (B-qemu-fix-647c2c33-pmmcheck-on-fullsuite.txt), which covers frees, not writes after free. On hardware the two
   PMM_CHECK boots walked the lists after every suite and found nothing (2 boots).

C. Read and set aside

- A reservation that starts inside a page, or free buddies left unmerged by pmm_init: both PMM_CHECK boots print
  "0 not page-aligned", "0 unmerged buddy pair(s)", "0 region(s) not page-aligned", and no firmware /memreserve/ entry.
- The fixed-address read at 0xBDE00000 (XHCI_SLOT3_HANDOFF_PHYS, kernel/drivers/usb/xhci/xhci_slot3_handoff.h:22;
  xhci.c:287-305): it is the start of the NC block, which the PMM does not manage (pmm_init ends region 1 at
  0xBDE00000), and the code only reads it.
- kexec's own copying (segments, purgatory, relocation code): finished before SLM-OS's first instruction; cannot write
  after pmm_init.
- The DTB blob inside the heap (#1244): reserved since 661204e6; the halt rate did not change.
- Camera RCE: held in reset by Linux's kexec teardown until SLM-OS starts it (project notes, #438); its buffers are
  SLM-OS allocations. BPMP/HSP/TCU shared memory: not verified to lie outside general RAM.

D. What the next image answers without any of this being assumed

slmos-1207-suite-647c2c33.elf walks the lists at the end of pmm_init, before the first suite, after every suite and once
a second, and prints the damaged block's address, its first 64 bytes, the 64 bytes before it and the time. The time
places the write before or after xhci_init (and the GPU bring-up) in the boot log; the address says which reservation
it sits next to; the bytes say what kind of data it is.
